# Project Hermes — Strategy

## The reframing

The project is not the product. Your **understanding, demonstrated publicly and
consistently**, is the product. Phoenix is the byproduct that proves it. This is why the
value compounds whether or not any single artifact sells — every piece feeds your
reputation, and reputation is what converts to income.

## Sequencing (do not skip stages)

The common mistake is leading with a paid product (book/course). That's the *hardest*
first sale — you compete with free official docs and established material. Instead,
demand pulls the product into existence. Sequence:

### Stage 1 — Visibility & leads (now → ongoing)
- **Primary money is not content — it's freelance DBA work.** Content's job is to make
  clients trust you before they meet you.
- **LinkedIn + short posts** = top of funnel. Cheap: one insight per sprint = one post.
  This is where clients and hiring managers actually are.
- Goal: a prospect who finds your posts and GitHub handbook hires you over an invisible
  competitor.

### Stage 2 — Credibility library (as sprints accumulate)
- **Hashnode first** (built-in audience, zero setup), **own-hosted blog later**
  (portfolio control, SEO, no platform risk).
- This is the "proof of depth" a serious client reads before signing.
- Monetizes indirectly via leads; slowly via SEO as it grows.

### Stage 3 — Reach & authority (when you have a back-catalogue)
- **YouTube.** Highest effort, slowest to pay, highest ceiling. DBA content is
  underserved there. Best trust-builder and funnel for a later paid product.

### Stage 4 — Capstone product (only after Stages 1–3 create demand)
- **Book or course**, assembled from content that already resonated (posts people
  engaged with, questions people repeatedly asked), sold to an audience you already
  built. Never attempt this first.

## Guardrails

- **Don't monetize yet.** For the first several sprints, publish freely, build the habit
  and the back-catalogue. The product emerges from noticing which posts land.
- **Don't over-build this repo.** With one sprint of source material, a README, this
  strategy, a source-map, and seeded ideas is enough. Platform folders fill as you
  actually publish. An elaborate empty structure is the same trap as an empty
  templates folder.
- **Content-rights caution.** Phoenix was AI-assisted; original prose is fine to
  commercialize. But any substantial text copied from official docs or books must be
  paraphrased or kept within fair-use quotation limits before it enters a paid product.
- **Open vs closed.** Consider opening Phoenix's learning content (builds audience) while
  keeping Hermes's premium output closed (that's what sells). Decide deliberately.

## The one metric that matters early

Not followers. Not views. **Replies, saves, and "how did you figure that out?" questions.**
Those signal which topics have pull — and pull is what tells you what to build in Stage 4.
